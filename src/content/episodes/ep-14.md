## Episode notes

In this episode of The Trades Show, host Briana Ottoboni sits down with Béatrice
Amblard, the only Hermès-trained leather artisan in the US working under her own
label and the founder of April in Paris and Heirloom Leathercraft, inside her
San Francisco atelier. Their conversation traces nearly five decades in leather
— from the moment a sixteen-year-old Béatrice, steered toward the trades by a
French school system that didn't know what to do with her dyslexia, walked into
a leather classroom and felt every one of her senses wake up — the scent of wax
and hide, the benches worn smooth by generations of students — to the four years
at Hermès that shaped her, where she learned that quality is non-negotiable and
that every stitch has a purpose.

Béatrice shares the philosophy behind her bespoke work — that real luxury is
quiet, singular, and made to be kept and passed down — and walks through the arc
of her career: leaving Hermès in 1988 to start April in Paris with nothing,
building a one-of-a-kind custom accessories practice, and being pushed into
teaching by students who would not take no for an answer, a leap that became
Heirloom Leathercraft, the only formal school of its kind in the country, where
she has trained more than a thousand people in fourteen years.

She is candid about the realities behind the romance — the tanneries that have
all but vanished, the quality of leather slipping as mass production took hold,
the physical toll that has her working fewer days, the guilt of burning a piece
that did not meet the mark — and genuinely hopeful about where craft and
technology meet, how AI can help a maker launch a business and how the
fast-paced tech world keeps sending her students hungry for something they can
make with their hands. Whether you are a maker, a small-business builder, or
someone craving a creative outlet beyond the screen, this episode is a
masterclass in building a bespoke business without compromising on quality,
preserving a trade by teaching it, and finding a whole life inside the work.

The Trades Show is an interview series on location with modern artisans
exploring what it means to be human: the ability to create with our hands.
