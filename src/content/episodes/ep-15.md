## Episode notes

In this episode of The Trades Show, host Briana Ottoboni sits down with Lorna
Kollmeyer, a sculptor, mold maker, and caster of ornamental plaster who owns the
last plaster shop in San Francisco, inside her studio at the Hunters Point
Shipyard. Their conversation traces how the whole trade fell into Lorna's lap —
from a young basketball star who landed in San Francisco in her early twenties
with no plan, enchanted by a city where no two Victorians looked alike, to the
couple restoring Victorian flats who taught her to strip originals and pour her
own molds, to the day someone asked if she wanted to buy a plaster business and
she answered, "No, but I'll ask my friends," before buying it herself.

Lorna walks through the Majestic Hotel — her very first job, a corner building
of mermaids and egg-and-dart she bid at twenty thousand dollars when the next
bid came in at fifty-six, learning sculpture from her girlfriends as they went —
and into what ornamental plaster actually is: the ceiling medallions, corbels,
and crown moldings that make San Francisco's architecture its own quirky self.
She opens up the process, from stripping a paint-caked original to laying rubber
and building a mother mold, and the archive it has become — thousands of pieces
brought in by homeowners who cared enough to save them, a living library she
calls the city's collection, not her own.

She's candid about the edges of the work — the ultra-wealthy gut-jobs that
hollow out a Victorian's interior, the plaster shops that closed as commercial
rents climbed, the fight to keep her shop and the Shipyard's artist community
from being knocked down for condos — and where the craft is going as 3D scanning
saves a three-month sculpting job and a new generation of thirty-somethings
tells her they're starving for things not spat out by a machine. Whether you're
a maker, a restorer, or someone who has ever loved an old house, this episode is
a masterclass in learning on the job, building a business that outlasts you, and
becoming the custodian of a city's history.

The Trades Show is an interview series on location with modern artisans
exploring what it means to be human: the ability to create with our hands.
